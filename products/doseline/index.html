Doseline

A shared record of what was prescribed and what was actually taken.

In development Early access open B2B
Request early access Book a walkthrough
Status

Doseline is in active development and is not yet generally available. This page describes what we are building and the problem it addresses. We are talking to clinics, care teams, and pharmacies who want to shape it — and we would rather hear from you now than after the design is set.

The problem

Between two appointments, a prescriber has almost no visibility into whether a patient took their medication. The conversation at the next visit relies on recall: how has it been going? — a question people answer optimistically, and often inaccurately, without meaning to mislead anyone.

The consequences compound quietly. A regimen that appears ineffective may simply be a regimen that was never followed. Doses get escalated in response to a problem that was actually a missed alarm, a confusing schedule, or a side effect the patient stopped taking the drug over and didn't mention. The information needed to tell these apart exists — it just never reaches the clinician in a usable form.

What Doseline does

Doseline creates one record that both sides can see. The clinician enters the regimen once. The patient gets a schedule on their phone and confirms each dose as they take it. The care team sees what actually happened, in time to do something about it.

Regimen, entered once

The prescriber defines the medication, dose, timing, and duration. No transcription by the patient, and no ambiguity about what was intended.

Confirmation, not self-report

Patients confirm doses as they take them, on a schedule that matches the prescription — rather than reconstructing three weeks from memory in a waiting room.

Adherence the team can see

A clear view per patient and across a caseload: what was taken, what was missed, and when the pattern started changing.

Flags that mean something

Attention is directed at sustained deviation from the regimen, not at every individual late dose. The goal is fewer alerts that matter more.

A reason attached

When a dose is missed, patients can say why in one tap — side effects, ran out, forgot, felt better. Context that changes the clinical response.

Built for handover

Designed for care that spans people: the covering clinician, the pharmacist, the family member helping at home.

Who we're building it for

  • Outpatient clinics managing chronic conditions where adherence determines whether a treatment plan works at all.
  • Care teams coordinating across prescriber, pharmacist, and caregiver, where today the record lives in three places or none.
  • Post-discharge programmes following patients through the weeks where medication regimens most often break down.
  • Clinical research coordinators who need adherence data that isn't a diary filled in retrospectively.

How we're approaching data

Medication data is among the most sensitive information a person has, and a B2B health product has to earn access to it. Our position is that patients should be able to see everything recorded about them, that data is shared with a named care team rather than an unbounded organisation, and that nothing is sold or used for advertising — the model we already apply across our consumer apps.

Important

Doseline is designed as a record-keeping and communication tool for patients and their care teams. It is not a medical device, it does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment, and it is not intended to replace clinical judgement or direct contact with a healthcare professional.

Regulatory posture, data processing agreements, and jurisdiction-specific compliance are being established as part of development. We will publish specifics rather than claims, and we're happy to discuss requirements directly with prospective partners.
